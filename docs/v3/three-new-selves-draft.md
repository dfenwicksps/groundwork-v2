### 1. Life story (Know myself)

| Field | Draft |
| --- | --- |
| Short (first person, as in the master) | The story I tell about how I got to be me. |
| Short (second person, as the app shows it) | The story you tell about how you got to be you. |
| What it is | The story you tell about how you became who you are: where you've come from, the moments that changed things, and where it seems to be heading. You're the one telling it, and you can tell it again differently as you learn more. |
| Not this | Not a list of everything that's happened to you, and not a script that's already written. Two people can live through the same thing and tell it differently. And not self-authorship: that's about checking your beliefs are your own. This is about how the pieces of your life fit together. |
| Ask yourself | If your life so far were a book, what would you call the chapter you're in now? |
| Living it | Ask someone who's known you a long time for one story about you that you don't remember. What does it add to yours? |
| Self-check statement | I can see how things I've been through have shaped who I am. |
| In Groundwork | Your Story So Far · Who Was I? · Where We Come From · The Through-Line |
| Teacher note | Habermas and Bluck (2000): the ability to tell a connected life story develops through adolescence. Students will vary a lot, and a short or patchy story at 13 is normal. Keep prompts on ordinary moments; don't ask for the hardest thing that's happened to them. |

### 2. Belonging (Value myself)

| Field | Draft |
| --- | --- |
| Short (first person) | Having people and places where I fit as I am. |
| Short (second person) | Having people and places where you fit as you are. |
| What it is | Feeling that you're part of something: a family, a friendship, a team, a class, a community. You're accepted there as you are, and you'd be missed if you weren't there. Most people have it in some places and not in others. |
| Not this | Not fitting in. Fitting in is changing yourself to be accepted; belonging is being accepted without having to. And not something you build on your own: it depends on the people around you as well. If you don't feel it somewhere, that doesn't mean something's wrong with you. |
| Ask yourself | Where do you feel most like you can just be yourself? Who's there? |
| Living it | Make it a bit easier for someone else to belong this week: save them a seat, ask them along, or use their name. |
| Self-check statement | There's at least one place where I can be myself and still feel part of things. |
| In Groundwork | Where You Belong · Fitting In vs. Belonging · Across the Gap · Reach Out Once |
| Teacher note | Baumeister and Leary (1995) describe belonging as a basic human need. Allen and colleagues (2018, 51 studies) found teacher support among the things most strongly linked to belonging at school. So this one is partly ours to build, not only the student's. |

### 3. Sense of purpose (Direct myself)

| Field | Draft |
| --- | --- |
| Short (first person) | Something I care about that's bigger than just me. |
| Short (second person) | Something you care about that's bigger than just you. |
| What it is | Something you care about enough to keep working at. It matters to you, and it does some good for someone or something beyond you. It can be big or small: a cause, a craft, the people you look after. |
| Not this | Not a career, and not something you're meant to have worked out by now. Plenty of adults are still finding theirs. And not a goal. A goal gets ticked off. A purpose is the reason you set it. |
| Ask yourself | What's something you'd keep caring about even if nobody gave you credit for it? |
| Living it | Do one small thing this week for something you care about. Notice how it feels compared with something you just had to do. |
| Self-check statement | There's something I care about that's bigger than just me. |
| In Groundwork | What Matters · What They Care About · The Contribution Map · Who Else Cares · Commitment Statement · One Real Step |
| Teacher note | Damon, Menon and Bronk (2003) define purpose as a lasting aim that matters to the person and makes a difference beyond them. Yeager and colleagues (2014) found that students who connected schoolwork to a purpose beyond themselves kept going longer at dull tasks. Most students won't have one yet, and exploring is the point. |

## 3. What I checked, and what I didn't

- **Checked this month:** Habermas & Bluck (2000), from a later paper by Habermas; Allen et al. (2018); Yeager et al. (2014). Details are in the review.
- **Checked on 17 November 2026:** Baumeister & Leary (1995), *Psychological Bulletin* 117(3), 497–529 ([Duke record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/677016)): the need to belong as a fundamental motivation, confirmed. Damon, Menon & Bronk (2003), *Applied Developmental Science* 7(3), 119–128 ([Stanford copy](https://coa.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/devofpurpose_0.pdf)): confirmed. Their definition is usually quoted as a stable and generalised intention that is meaningful to the self and consequential beyond it; I confirmed that from later papers citing it, not the 2003 text. The teacher note's wording ("a lasting aim that matters to the person and makes a difference beyond them") is a fair paraphrase.
- The line "Plenty of adults are still finding theirs" is a plain statement, not a research finding.
- "Fitting in is changing yourself to be accepted" follows the existing activity Fitting In vs. Belonging.

## 5. One placement question

Sense of purpose would sit in Direct myself, but its six activities are spread across three levels:

| Activity | Level now |
| --- | --- |
| What Matters · What They Care About | Manage myself |
| The Contribution Map · Who Else Cares · One Real Step | Believe in myself |
| Commitment Statement | Direct myself |

Two ways to handle it:

- **A. Move What Matters and What They Care About to Direct myself.** The first map marked both as "could move" there. Direct myself would then hold three of the six, and Manage myself would be left with two activities until the 13 November drafts are approved.
- **B. Leave every activity where it is**, and let the Sense of purpose page list all six wherever they sit. This needs the build that ties each activity to its self (recommendation 4 in the review).

I'd do B. It moves nothing, and it's the same build that fixes the same problem for every other self.
