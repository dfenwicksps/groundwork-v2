## 2. Rules these drafts follow

The same as the 13 November drafts:

- **Same shape as a v2 step:** an introduction, one prompt, three guided questions with tap-to-start options, a wrap-up. They'd run on the existing writing screen, so "Need to talk?", the support card and the gentle check apply unchanged.
- **No scores, ratings, labels or rankings.** Nothing asks a student to rate themselves.
- **No streaks or counts.** "Try it" means once, with one reflection.
- **Everyday situations only.** No question asks about risk, harm or anything clinical.
- **Each stands alone.** None needs another activity done first.
- **No stories or media attached.** Only a person adds those.
- **Research stays out of the student wording.** The evidence is in section 4.

One more for this set: **each activity practises the self it's named for.** A student who finishes it has done the thing once, not read about it.

## 3. The drafts

### 1. Name It (Know myself, self-awareness)

**Introduction.** Most of the time we use about four words for how we feel: good, bad, stressed, fine. But "stressed" might be worried, or embarrassed, or left out, or just tired. Those need different things. The closer the word, the easier it is to know what to do next.

**Prompt.** Pick one moment from the last day or two when you felt something, and find the closest word for it.

| Guided question | Options to start from |
| --- | --- |
| What was the moment? Keep it ordinary. | Getting a mark back · Something someone said · Waiting for a reply · Walking into a room · Being told to do something |
| What was the first word you'd have used for the feeling? | Annoyed · Stressed · Fine · Bad · Weird |
| Is there a closer word underneath it? | Embarrassed · Left out · Disappointed · Worried about what's next · Jealous · Tired · Proud, but didn't want to show it · Relieved |

**Try it.** Once more this week, when you notice a feeling, stop and look for the closer word. Come back and write which word it was.

**Wrap-up.** You went from a rough word to a closer one. That's the whole skill, and it gets quicker. You don't have to do anything about the feeling. Knowing what it is comes first.

### 2. The Debrief (Know myself, self-reflection)

**Introduction.** There are two ways to think about something that didn't go well. One goes round and round and leaves you feeling worse. The other takes three questions and ends with something to try. This is the second one.

**Prompt.** Pick one thing from this week that didn't go to plan, and run it through three questions.

| Guided question | Options to start from |
| --- | --- |
| What happened? Just the facts, the way a friend who was there would tell it. | A test or assignment didn't go well · I had an argument · I said something I wish I hadn't · I didn't do something I meant to · I lost or played badly |
| What was going on for you at the time? | I was tired · I was rushing · I was nervous · I was annoyed about something else · I hadn't prepared · I wanted to impress someone |
| What's one thing you'd try next time? Small is fine. | Start earlier · Say it differently · Ask before assuming · Take a minute first · Get help sooner |

**Wrap-up.** That's a debrief: what happened, what was going on, what next. Then you stop. If you catch yourself going back over it again, you've already got your answer, and you can let it go.

### 3. Three Words (Know myself, self-image)

**Introduction.** Everyone carries a picture of how they come across. Most of it is guesswork, and the guesses are often harder on us than other people are. The only way to check is to ask.

**Prompt.** Guess the three words someone would use to describe you. Then ask them.

| Guided question | Options to start from |
| --- | --- |
| Before you ask: what three words do you think they'd use? | Quiet · Funny · Loud · Serious · Kind · Stubborn · Sporty · Smart · Awkward |
| Ask one person you trust for three words they'd use for you. What did they say? | A parent or carer · A friend · A brother or sister · A coach or teacher |
| What matched, and what surprised you? | They saw something I didn't · They didn't mention what I worry about · It mostly matched · One word I want to think about |

**Wrap-up.** Now your picture has a bit less guesswork in it. One person's three words aren't the whole truth either. But you checked, and that's better than guessing.

### 4. Getting Better At (Value myself, self-esteem)

**Introduction.** How you rate yourself goes up and down, and that's normal. You can't just decide to rate yourself higher, and being told you're great doesn't do much. What does move it, slowly, is getting better at things that matter to you. So this activity isn't about how you feel about yourself. It's about noticing where you're improving.

**Prompt.** Pick one thing that matters to you, and look at where you are with it compared with a year ago.

| Guided question | Options to start from |
| --- | --- |
| What's something you care about being good at? It doesn't have to be a school thing. | A sport · An instrument or art · A subject · Being a good friend · Cooking, building or fixing things · Looking after someone |
| What can you do in it now that you couldn't a year ago? | I'm faster or stronger · I understand more of it · I get nervous less · I can do it without help · People ask me for help with it |
| What's the next small thing you'd like to be able to do? | One skill I've been avoiding · Doing it in front of people · Doing it more often · Helping someone else learn it |

**Wrap-up.** That's real, and you did it. On a week when you're rating yourself low, this is worth coming back to. Not as a pep talk. As a fact.

### 5. Not My Results (Value myself, self-worth)

**Introduction.** Some weeks go well and some don't. If your sense of being worth something goes up and down with them, every bad result feels like it's about you. This activity looks at the parts of you that don't depend on how the week went.

**Prompt.** Name some things about you that would still be true after your worst week.

| Guided question | Options to start from |
| --- | --- |
| What's something you care about that has nothing to do with marks, wins or likes? | My family · My friends · Being fair · My faith or beliefs · Animals · Making people laugh · Music |
| What's something about how you treat people that stays the same whether you're winning or losing? | I stick up for people · I listen · I turn up · I include people · I keep my word |
| Who is someone who'd think the same of you after a bad week? | A parent or carer · A grandparent · A friend · A pet, honestly · I'm not sure right now |

**Wrap-up.** None of that changed last week, and none of it will change next week. Results matter, and it's fine to care about them. They're just not the bit that makes you count.

*If the last answer was "I'm not sure right now", the wrap-up adds:* That's more common than you'd think, and it can change. Your support circle is a good place to start.

### 6. What I'd Say to a Friend (Value myself, self-compassion)

**Introduction.** When a friend stuffs something up, most of us know what to say. When we do it ourselves, we say things we'd never say to them. This activity borrows the voice you already use for other people.

**Prompt.** Pick a small, ordinary stuff-up from lately, and answer it the way you'd answer a friend.

| Guided question | Options to start from |
| --- | --- |
| What's a small thing you got wrong lately? Keep it everyday. | I forgot something · I said the wrong thing · I did badly at something · I let someone down in a small way · I wasted time I needed |
| What did you say to yourself about it? | "Typical" · "I always do this" · "Everyone saw" · "I'm useless at this" · Not much, I just felt bad |
| If a friend told you they'd done exactly the same thing, what would you say to them? | "Everyone does that" · "It's one time" · "You were tired" · "What do you need now?" · "It's fixable" |

**Wrap-up.** Read back what you'd say to your friend. It's true for you too. Being kind to yourself isn't letting yourself off. It just makes it easier to have another go.

### 7. My Reason (Manage myself, self-motivation)

**Introduction.** There are things you do because you want to and things you do because you have to. The second kind is hard to start. It gets easier when you can find a reason that's actually yours, even a small one. Not the reason you've been given. Yours.

**Prompt.** Pick one thing you have to do this week and find your own reason for it.

| Guided question | Options to start from |
| --- | --- |
| What's one thing you have to do this week that you're not keen on? | An assignment · Study for a test · Training · A job at home · Something I said I'd do |
| What reason have you been given for it? | It's due · It's on the test · I was told to · It's my turn · I don't know |
| What could it do for you, or for someone you care about? | It gets me closer to something I want · It's a skill I'll use · Someone's counting on me · I'll feel better once it's done · It's practice at doing hard things |

**Wrap-up.** Write your reason in one line and put it where you'll see it before you start. It won't make the thing fun. It makes it yours, and that's usually enough to begin.

### 8. You Are Here (Imagine myself, actual self)

**Introduction.** Before you can think about who you might become, you need a pin on the map for where you are now. Not the version you show people, and not the one you wish you were. Just a fair description of today.

**Prompt.** Describe who you are right now, the way a fair friend would.

| Guided question | Options to start from |
| --- | --- |
| What are three things that are true about you at the moment? | I'm loyal · I'm curious · I get nervous in groups · I work hard at things I like · I'm funny with people I know · I'm still working out what I'm into |
| What's something you do most weeks that says something about you? | I look after someone · I train or practise · I make things · I read or watch a lot about one topic · I help out at home |
| What's one thing you're working on? | Speaking up · Being more organised · Staying calmer · Trying new things · Being a better friend |

**Wrap-up.** That's your pin. It's not a score and it's not permanent. It's just where you're starting from, and a starting point is useful.

### 9. Should or Choose? (Imagine myself, ought self)

**Introduction.** Everyone carries a list of things they feel they should be: from family, friends, school, their culture, what they see online. Some of those you'd choose anyway. Some you're just carrying. This activity sorts them. Keeping one is as good an answer as dropping one.

**Prompt.** Take a few "I should" sentences and work out which are yours.

| Guided question | Options to start from |
| --- | --- |
| Finish this sentence a few times: "I should…" | be better at school · be more outgoing · be good at sport · look a certain way · know what I want to do · be easier to get along with |
| Pick one. Where does it come from? | My family · My friends · School · Things I see online · My culture or faith · Me · I'm not sure |
| Try saying it as "I choose to…". Does it still sound right? | Yes, I'd choose it anyway · Partly · I'd choose a smaller version · No, it's not really mine · I don't know yet |

**Wrap-up.** Some of your "shoulds" turned out to be yours. Those are easier to carry now. If one of them isn't, you don't have to do anything about it today. It might be worth talking over with someone you trust.

### 10. The Other Version (Imagine myself, feared self)

**Introduction.** Most people have a version of themselves they don't want to turn into. Someone who gave up on things, or stopped being kind, or went along with everyone. Thinking about that version isn't a bad sign. It's useful, because it shows you what you care about. It works best when you put the opposite beside it.

**Prompt.** Name one version of you that you'd rather not become, and the version you'd rather be.

| Guided question | Options to start from |
| --- | --- |
| In everyday terms, what's one version of you that you don't want to become? | Someone who gives up when it's hard · Someone who's unkind to get a laugh · Someone who never tries anything · Someone who lets friends down · Someone who just goes along with it |
| What's the opposite version, the one you'd want? | Someone who sticks at things · Someone people feel safe around · Someone who has a go · Someone you can count on · Someone who says what they think |
| What's one small thing this week that the second version would do? | Finish one thing I'd usually drop · Say something kind I'd usually keep in · Try one new thing · Keep one promise · Say what I think once |

**Wrap-up.** The version you don't want just told you what you do want. That's all it's for. One small thing in the right direction counts for more than worrying about the wrong one.

## 4. The evidence, and what I couldn't confirm

Checked on 16 November 2026. "Confirmed" means I found the publication and its main finding. It doesn't mean any of these activities has itself been tested. None has.

| Activity | Rests on | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Name It | Telling feelings apart precisely is at its lowest in the mid-teens. Nook et al. (2018), *Psychological Science* 29(8). | **Confirmed** for the finding. That practising it helps teenagers is my inference; I found no trial of an exercise like this one. |
| 2. The Debrief | Reflection and rumination are different (Trapnell & Campbell, 1999). Stepping back from an upsetting event goes with less distress in adolescents (White, Kross & Duckworth, 2015). Writing about experiences has a small positive effect in ages 10 to 18 (Travagin et al., 2015, g = 0.13). | **Confirmed**, with a limit: the White study observed what teenagers did on their own; it didn't teach them. |
| 3. Three Words | People underestimate how well they come across (Boothby et al., 2018, in adults; Wolf et al., 2021, in children). Close others know some things about us better than we do (Vazire, 2010). | **Confirmed** for the findings. No source for this exercise. |
| 4. Getting Better At | Self-esteem follows competence in areas a person values (Harter). Programs aimed at a specific area work better than general ones (O'Mara et al., 2006). | **Confirmed** for O'Mara (earlier review). Harter is cited from memory. |
| 5. Not My Results | Worth that depends on results is linked to more problems (Crocker & Luhtanen, 2003). Writing about values that matter to you ("values affirmation"). | **Confirmed** for Crocker (earlier review). Values affirmation **not re-checked**; my understanding is that school results are mixed. |
| 6. What I'd Say to a Friend | Self-compassion training has moderate effects across 27 trials, mostly adults (Ferrari et al., 2019). A small trial with 14 to 17-year-olds was promising (Bluth et al., 2016). The friend comparison is a standard exercise from Neff's work. | **Confirmed** for both studies. Those were multi-week courses, not one activity. |
| 7. My Reason | Students who wrote about how their science class related to their own lives gained in interest and grades, if they started with low expectations (Hulleman & Harackiewicz, 2009). A reason that reaches beyond yourself helped students persist at dull work (Yeager et al., 2014). | **Confirmed** from later reviews and the abstract. |
| 8. You Are Here | The actual self as the reference point in Higgins (1987). | **Confirmed** as theory. No source for an exercise. |
| 9. Should or Choose? | The ought self (Higgins, 1987). Taking on an expectation as your own is linked to better outcomes than acting from pressure (Ryan & Deci, 2000). | **Confirmed** as theory. **No tested exercise found.** Higgins's theory predicts that dwelling on the gap raises anxiety, so the draft sorts expectations and doesn't measure a gap. |
| 10. The Other Version | A feared self helps most when balanced by a hoped-for self in the same area (Oyserman & Markus, 1990). In a school trial, linking possible selves to things to do now improved grades and attendance (Oyserman, Bybee & Terry, 2006). | **Confirmed.** The 2006 program ran for 11 sessions; one activity is much less. |

## 5. Things for David to decide

1. **The wording,** line by line.
2. **Keeping 6, 9 and 10 from the AI.** A stuff-up, a family expectation and a feared version of yourself can all be personal. I've proposed they're private, like *Parts of Who You Are*, which means no AI follow-up on them.
3. **Counsellor review of 5, 6, 9 and 10 before any student sees them.** These are the four where an everyday prompt could bring up something heavier. Each keeps to ordinary examples and runs on the screen that has "Need to talk?". That isn't the same as a counsellor having read them.
4. **The extra line in Not My Results.** It appears only when a student taps "I'm not sure right now". It follows the Mattering page's wording, which David approved earlier. It needs building as a conditional line, and it needs his approval here.
5. **Getting Better At is indirect on purpose.** The research says self-esteem isn't built by working on self-esteem. If David wants an activity that addresses it head on, I'd advise against it.
6. **You Are Here overlaps Identity Letter.** Both describe who I am now. This one is shorter and sets up the rest of Imagine myself. If that's too close, the alternative is no activity for Actual self and a line on its page pointing to Know myself.
7. **Three Words uses the Self-image page's existing try-it.** If this becomes an activity, the page needs a different try-it or none.
