# Groundwork v3: where the missions and weeks would go in seven levels

**Status: agreed. David answered the questions on 13 Nov 2026 (section 9, D52) and approved the grouping and four moves in section 10 (D53). The app's map is `src/lib/v3/levelMap.ts`, which has the four moves applied; the tables in section 3 show the placements before them.**

David's idea is that the seven levels become the one structure of the program, and the four missions and the 10-week character program are folded into them. This page places every existing piece in a level so the idea can be judged against the real content.

## 1. How to read this

- **28 mission steps and 10 program weeks** are placed, each once. Nothing is left out and nothing is rewritten.
- Each piece is matched to the self it builds most, using the one-line description of that self in the master file. **This is my judgement from reading the content. It hasn't been checked against research, and it's yours to correct.**
- **Sure** means the piece is plainly about that self. **Could move** means it fits two levels and I've picked one; the other is named.
- The levels stay a map, not a ladder (D3). A number is a label, not an order.

Mission 1 moves whole into Level 1. Mission 3 splits between Levels 1 and 2. **Mission 2 (Purpose) is scattered across four levels**, and its arc (name what matters, link it to you, find your people, commit, act) no longer reads as one journey. Mission 4 mostly becomes Levels 6 and 7.

The program spreads evenly: every level gets at least one week.

## 5. What this shows

1. **The levels are uneven.** (David has since decided to write new activities first, D51; drafts are in [new-activities-draft.md](new-activities-draft.md).) Level 1 has 14 pieces; Level 4 has 2. Either Levels 3, 4 and 5 get new activities, or they open thin. Each self still has its own page from the master file (what it is, a story, something to try), so no level is empty, but those pages are drafts with gaps (see [content-gaps.md](content-gaps.md)).
2. **Six selves have no existing activity of their own:** self-esteem, self-compassion, self-confidence, self-advocacy, actual self and ought self. New activities would be new content, written by a person. Self-check rules still apply: no scores, no risk-language questions.
3. **Purpose loses its home.** If "What do I care about?" matters as a thread, it could stay as a theme that runs across Levels 2 to 5 instead of disappearing.
4. **The four milestone letters land in Levels 1, 2, 5 and 6.** Levels 3, 4 and 7 have none. The four "talk to someone" conversations land in Levels 1 (two), 3 and 6.

## 7. Wording that would have to change

This is the main cost in existing content. All of it needs your approval line by line; none of it touches the safety wording.

- **Every step's subtitle** says "Step 2 of 7" or similar. Those go.
- **Many step introductions** refer to the mission order: "Mission 1 looked inward. This one looks outward", "In Mission 1 you named some chapters", "Four missions of understanding".
- **Four steps are all called "Mission Challenge".** They need their own names.
- **Week 10** opens with "Nine weeks of evidence… and four missions".
- **The master file's poster** says "Level 1 at the bottom and level up" and calls Level 7 the "final level". That's ladder language, and D3 says the levels are a map.

## 10. Grouping the levels under the four names (approved, D53)

Yes, with one weak spot. The four names become four groups on the map, each keeping its question:

| Group | Its question | Levels in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Who am I? | Know myself |
| Connection | Where do I belong? | Value myself |
| Purpose | What do I care about? | Manage myself, Believe in myself, Direct myself |
| Meaning | What kind of life do I want? | Imagine myself, Authentic self |

**How well it fits.** As the map stands, 21 of the 28 mission steps already sit in the group that carries their old mission's name. Four moves, all of them placements already marked "could move", lift that to 25:

| Piece | From | To | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who Else Cares | Value myself | Believe in myself | David named this as one of its two homes. It keeps Purpose together. |
| The Contribution Map | Value myself | Believe in myself | "What could I bring?" is about what you can do. Keeps Purpose together. |
| Across the Gap | Know myself | Value myself | A Connection step, back with the others. |
| Digital Self | Know myself | Imagine myself | Becomes the one activity for the actual self, which had none. |

The three that still sit outside their old name are Where We Come From and Culture and Heritage (Connection steps in Know myself) and Where You've Come From (a Meaning step in Direct myself). Each fits its level better than its old mission, so I'd leave them.

**The count after those moves and the eight new activities:**

| Group | Level | Pieces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Know myself | 12 |
| Connection | Value myself | 7 |
| Purpose | Manage myself | 6 |
| | Believe in myself | 7 |
| | Direct myself | 6 |
| Meaning | Imagine myself | 5 |
| | Authentic self | 3 |

**The weak spot.** Value myself under "Where do I belong?" fits mattering and the belonging steps. It doesn't fit self-esteem, self-worth and self-compassion nearly as well: those are about how you regard yourself, not about other people. This is my reading, not a research finding.

**The other way to do it.** Leave the levels ungrouped and tag each piece with the name of the mission it came from, as a thread that runs across levels. That's exact, but it gives students two ways of sorting the same things. I'd recommend the grouping above, because it's one picture.

**Purpose is the largest group** (19 of 46 pieces, three of seven levels). That follows from where the levels put motivation, belief and choice, and it suits the name.
