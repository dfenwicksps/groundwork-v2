# What each level is made of, and whether the activities build it

**Date:** 16 November 2026
**Asked for by David:** "In Know Myself, I'm saying to know yourself is made up of Self-awareness, self-reflection, self-concept, self-image and social self. Firstly, I need research supporting this and a review to determine if I'm missing anything. Second, activities need to specifically build that self in the person. Same for Connection, Purpose and Meaning."

**Status:** a review. Nothing in the app has changed. Every recommendation is David's to decide (section 8).

**How to read the evidence labels**
- **Strong:** meta-analyses, or large longitudinal or randomised studies.
- **Moderate:** consistent studies, mostly correlational or small trials.
- **Thin:** little research, a disputed construct, or evidence only in adults.
- "Checked" means I found the publication and its main finding this month or for an earlier Groundwork review. Section 9 lists what was checked and what wasn't.

---

## 1. The short answer

1. **No published framework lists these selves under these seven headings.** Each level's list is put together from several separate research traditions. That is a fair way to build a teaching vocabulary, and most of the 25 selves are real research constructs. It does mean the app and the school materials shouldn't say "research shows knowing yourself is made up of these five". They can say each self is drawn from research, and name it.
2. **Know myself is the best supported list.** Three of its five selves match the three parts of Self-awareness in the Australian Curriculum (personal awareness, emotional awareness, reflective practice), and four of the five match Neisser's kinds of self-knowledge.
3. **One thing is missing from three of the four groups, and it is the same kind of thing each time.** All 25 selves face inward: they are about how I see, rate, steer or picture *myself*. Three of the four group questions face outward: *Where do I belong? What do I care about? What kind of life do I want?* So the map has no self for:
   - **my story** (research name: narrative identity), which Know myself and Meaning both lean on;
   - **belonging**, which is Connection's own question;
   - **purpose**, which is the Purpose group's own name.
4. **That gap is why the activities and the selves don't line up.** Of the 38 activities on the map, 13 were written mainly for something the map has no self for (section 6.3). Twelve of the 13 are about story, belonging or purpose. They are good activities sitting under selves they weren't written for.
5. **Counting the other way, 8 of the 25 selves have no activity that builds them**, and 5 more are only touched. Three of the 8 are covered by the eight drafts from 13 November (still unapproved). Ten selves still needed a draft; those are now in [self-activities-draft.md](../v3/self-activities-draft.md).
6. **For five selves the research on how to build them is thin**: self-trust, self-respect, self-image, self-confidence as something separate from self-efficacy, and self-authorship in school-age students. Activities for these rest on reasoning, not on tested exercises.

## 2. Know myself (Identity: "Who am I?")

*Self-awareness · Self-reflection · Self-concept · Self-image · Social self*

### 2.1 What research says knowing yourself is made of

| Framework | Its parts | How the five selves match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Australian Curriculum v9, Personal and Social capability.** Its Self-awareness element has three sub-elements. | Personal awareness, emotional awareness, reflective practice | Self-concept, self-awareness, self-reflection. A close match, and the one schools will recognise. |
| **Neisser (1988), five kinds of self-knowledge.** | Ecological (the body in its surroundings), interpersonal, extended (memory and anticipation), private, conceptual | Social self = interpersonal. Self-awareness = private. Self-concept = conceptual. **Nothing matches the extended self**: me across time. |
| **Private and public self-consciousness** (Fenigstein, Scheier & Buss, 1975), refined by Trapnell & Campbell (1999) and Grant, Franklin & Langford (2002). | Attention to my inner states; attention to how I appear to others; reflection; insight | Self-awareness and self-reflection (private side); self-image and social self (public side). |
| **The looking-glass self** (Cooley, 1902), tested by Shrauger & Schoeneman (1979). | How I think others see me shapes how I see myself | Social self and self-image. |
| **Vazire (2010), who knows what about a person.** | Some things I know best; some things people close to me know better | Supports having both an inward and an outward route to knowing yourself. |

### 2.2 Each self

| Self | Research standing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-awareness | Moderate | Naming feelings precisely ("emotion differentiation") is measurably low in the mid-teens (Nook et al., 2018), so teaching it at this age is well aimed. |
| Self-reflection | Moderate | Reflection and rumination are different (Trapnell & Campbell, 1999). Reflecting a lot is not the same as gaining insight (Grant et al., 2002). The app's page already makes the first point well. |
| Self-concept | Strong | The best-established construct in the level. |
| Self-image | Thin as a separate construct | In research it is part of self-concept, or means body image. The app's meaning (how I think I come across) is closest to "meta-perception". People's guesses here are often wrong and often too harsh (Shrauger & Schoeneman, 1979; Boothby et al., 2018). |
| Social self | Moderate | Going back to James (1890) and Cooley. Teenagers describing themselves differently in different rooms, and being bothered by it, is normal from about Year 9 (Harter & Monsour, 1992). |

### 2.3 What's missing

| Gap | Why it belongs here | What the app already has | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **My story** (narrative identity; Neisser's extended self) | Knowing who I am includes knowing how I got here. The ability to tell a connected life story develops through adolescence (Habermas & Bluck, 2000). | Your Story So Far, Who Was I?, Where We Come From, and My Story. Three activities and a feature, and no self. | **Add a self for it.** The strongest gap in the level. It could sit here or in Meaning (section 5.3). |
| **The groups I'm part of** (cultural, family, faith, community identity) | Social self, as the page defines it, is who I am with other people and how they see me. It isn't which groups I belong to. Research treats these separately (personal, relational and collective identity). An earlier review confirmed the Identity Project trial on ethnic-racial identity. | Culture and Heritage, Where We Come From, part of Parts of Who You Are. | **Either widen Social self's wording to include it, or add a self.** Widening is the smaller change. |
| Checking my picture against other people's | My own view has blind spots (Vazire, 2010). | The Self-image try-it and the three "talk to someone" activities do this. | No new self. Worth keeping as a design rule: every level has one activity that asks someone else. |

Not missing: values and strengths (they are the content of self-concept), and self-concept clarity (an outcome the self-check looks at, not something to teach as a self).

### 2.4 Do the activities build each self?

| Self | What research suggests builds it | What the level has now | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Self-awareness | Practice at noticing a feeling and naming it precisely | Nothing. Strengths Mapping was filed under it in the first map, but it builds self-concept. The self page's try-it (name the feeling under the feeling) is on target. | **No activity.** Draft: *Name It*. |
| Self-reflection | A short, structured look back that ends in something to try; looking at it as if it happened to someone else (White, Kross & Duckworth, 2015) | The journal and "Do I leave room for inner work?" make room for reflecting. Neither teaches how. | **Touched.** Draft: *The Debrief*. |
| Self-concept | Describing yourself in specific areas (O'Mara et al., 2006) | Strengths Mapping, Values Clarifier, Identity Letter, Parts of Who You Are | **Built.** The best-covered self on the map. |
| Self-image | Comparing your guess with what people actually say | Nothing. Digital Self was moved to Imagine myself in D53. | **No activity.** Draft: *Three Words*. Or move Digital Self back. |
| Social self | Looking at the versions of you in different rooms | The Mask Check, Who Was I?, Who is shaping me? | **Built.** |

Activities in this level that build none of the five: Your Story So Far, Where We Come From, Culture and Heritage. All three would have a home if the gaps above are filled. Live It Once builds whichever self the student chooses to act on.

**A caution.** Don't build self-awareness as a mindfulness course. The largest school trial (MYRIAD, 85 UK schools, ages 11 to 14) found no benefit to students' mental health, and a companion paper suggested it may be unhelpful for students who already have symptoms. Naming feelings is a much smaller thing than that programme, but the app shouldn't claim more for it than the evidence gives.

## 3. Value myself (Connection: "Where do I belong?")

*Self-esteem · Self-worth · Mattering · Self-respect · Self-compassion*

### 3.1 What research says valuing yourself is made of

| Framework | Its parts | How the five selves match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rosenberg (1965); Harter.** | Global self-esteem, fed by competence in areas I care about and by other people's regard | Self-esteem. Researchers use "self-worth" for the same thing. |
| **Crocker (contingent self-worth); Kernis (secure and fragile self-esteem).** | Worth that depends on results, against worth that doesn't | The app's esteem and worth split. A teaching device built on these, as the earlier review said. |
| **Sociometer theory** (Leary & Baumeister, 2000). | Self-esteem works as a gauge of how accepted and valued I am by others | **This supports putting self-esteem under Connection.** In D53 I called that the weak spot of the grouping. For self-esteem and mattering, the research says the grouping is sound. |
| **Mattering** (Rosenberg & McCullough, 1981; Prilleltensky, 2020). | Feeling valued, and adding value | Mattering. |
| **Self-compassion** (Neff). | Self-kindness, common humanity, mindfulness | Self-compassion. |
| **Self-respect** (Dillon in philosophy; Renger, 2018, in psychology). | Seeing myself as having the same rights and standing as anyone else | Self-respect. Renger found it predicts speaking up, not aggression. |

### 3.2 Each self

| Self | Research standing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-esteem | Strong, with small effects | Not built directly. It follows from competence in what I value and from being accepted. Praise is the wrong tool (covered in the earlier review). |
| Self-worth | Moderate | See above: same word as self-esteem in most research. |
| Mattering | Moderate | The construct closest to what Groundwork's character program does. |
| Self-respect | Thin | Renger's definition (equal standing) differs a little from the app's (standing by my values and boundaries). The app's version leans toward moral identity. |
| Self-compassion | Moderate to strong in adults, moderate in teenagers | A meta-analysis of 27 trials found moderate effects, mostly in adults (Ferrari et al., 2019). A small teen trial was promising (Bluth et al., 2016). |

### 3.3 What's missing

| Gap | Why it belongs here | What the app already has | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Belonging** | It is the group's own question, and none of the five selves is belonging. The page for Mattering even says "Not belonging". Belonging is one of the best-supported needs in psychology (Baumeister & Leary, 1995; relatedness in Ryan & Deci, 2000). | Where You Belong, Fitting In vs. Belonging, Reach Out Once | **Add a self for it.** |
| **How I relate to other people** (empathy, seeing another view) | Every self in Connection is about how I regard myself. The Australian Curriculum's Social awareness element (empathy, relational awareness, community awareness) has no counterpart on the map. | Across the Gap; Who Else Cares | **Decide whether it's in scope.** The map is a map of selves, so leaving this to the activities is defensible. If belonging is added, its page could carry this. |

**A caution about belonging.** A meta-analysis of 51 studies found that teacher support was among the strongest things linked to a student's sense of belonging at school (Allen et al., 2018). Belonging is mostly given by the people around a student. A page or activity about it shouldn't read as though a student who doesn't feel they belong has failed to build something.

### 3.4 Do the activities build each self?

| Self | What research suggests builds it | What the level has now | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Self-esteem | Indirectly: getting better at something I care about, and being accepted | Nothing | **No activity.** Draft: *Getting Better At*. It is indirect on purpose. |
| Self-worth | Writing about what matters to me apart from results (values affirmation; findings in schools are mixed, and not re-checked this time); regard from people who know me | The People Who Shaped You | **Touched.** Draft: *Not My Results*. |
| Mattering | Doing things for others (Layous et al., 2012, ages 9 to 11; Fuligni, 2019) | Am I a contributor?, Reach Out Once | **Built.** |
| Self-respect | No tested exercise found | What do I stand for?, Fitting In vs. Belonging | **Built**, on reasoning. |
| Self-compassion | Speaking to yourself as you would to a friend; writing to yourself kindly | Nothing on the map. The hard-on-yourself card responds when a student is harsh; it doesn't teach the skill. | **No activity.** Draft: *What I'd Say to a Friend*. |

Activities in this level that build none of the five: Where You Belong and Across the Gap. Both are about belonging and relating.

## 4. Purpose ("What do I care about?")

*Manage myself:* Self-regulation · Self-discipline · Self-motivation
*Believe in myself:* Self-efficacy · Self-confidence · Self-trust
*Direct myself:* Autonomy · Self-advocacy · Self-authorship

### 4.1 What research says purpose is made of

| Framework | Its parts | How the nine selves match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Purpose** (Damon, Menon & Bronk, 2003; not re-checked). | A lasting aim that matters to me **and** makes a difference beyond me | **None of the nine.** See 4.3. |
| **Human agency** (Bandura, 2006). | Intentionality, forethought, self-reactiveness (steering myself), self-reflectiveness (judging my own ability) | Self-regulation and self-discipline = self-reactiveness. Self-efficacy = self-reflectiveness. Autonomy = intentionality. **Forethought (planning ahead) has no self.** |
| **Self-Determination Theory** (Ryan & Deci, 2000). | Autonomy, competence, relatedness | Direct myself, Believe in myself, and the Connection group. The three levels here and Connection line up with the three needs. This is the best single piece of support for the map's overall shape. |
| **Hope theory** (Snyder). | Goals, pathways, agency | Believe in myself covers agency. Goals and pathways have no self. |
| **Australian Curriculum, Self-management.** | I could confirm emotional regulation and perseverance. I couldn't confirm the full list. | Self-regulation and self-discipline. |

So the nine selves are a sound account of **the capacity to pursue something**: steering, believing and choosing. They are not an account of purpose itself.

### 4.2 Each self

| Self | Research standing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-regulation | Strong | |
| Self-discipline | Strong, but it overlaps | Duckworth uses self-discipline and self-control for the same thing. The app's split (in the moment, against over time) is workable as teaching. Lasting discipline comes more from set-up and routine than from effort (Duckworth, Gendler & Gross, 2016). |
| Self-motivation | Strong for motivation; the word itself isn't a research construct | Finding a reason that is mine. |
| Self-efficacy | Strong | |
| Self-confidence | Thin as something separate | The everyday word for self-efficacy (earlier review). |
| Self-trust | **None found** | Flagged on 13 November. It is the app's own idea. |
| Autonomy | Strong | |
| Self-advocacy | Moderate | The framework comes from studies of students with disabilities (Test et al., 2005). |
| Self-authorship | Thin for school-age students | Baxter Magolda's research followed university students into their thirties. |

### 4.3 What's missing

| Gap | Why it belongs here | What the app already has | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Purpose itself**: what I care about, and what I could add beyond myself | It is the group's name and question, and no self carries it. | What Matters, What They Care About, The Contribution Map, Who Else Cares, Commitment Statement, One Real Step. **Six activities, the whole of the old Purpose mission, with no self.** | **Add a self for it.** The clearest gap on the whole map. |
| **Planning ahead** (goals, and ways to reach them) | Bandura's forethought; Snyder's pathways. | Goals and habits on Me; Next Chapter | No new self. Could be written into Self-regulation's or Autonomy's page, and it is what the *If-Then Plan* draft does. |

### 4.4 Do the activities build each self?

| Self | What research suggests builds it | What the level has now | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Self-regulation | Deciding in advance what I'll do in the hard moment (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006) | Can I manage myself? (three "I'm the kind of person who" statements) | **Touched.** The 13 November draft *The If-Then Plan* is on target. |
| Self-discipline | Changing the set-up; routines | Do I do hard things? | **Built.** *Make It Easier* (13 November) adds the set-up side. |
| Self-motivation | Writing my own reason for a task (Hulleman & Harackiewicz, 2009); a reason that reaches beyond me (Yeager et al., 2014) | What Matters and What They Care About name what a student cares about. Neither ties a reason to something they have to do. | **Touched.** Draft: *My Reason*. |
| Self-efficacy | Small real successes; seeing someone like me do it | One Real Step | **Built**, lightly. *The Proof File* (13 November) strengthens it. |
| Self-confidence | The same as self-efficacy | Nothing | **No activity.** *The Smallest Version* (13 November). |
| Self-trust | No evidence base | Can people trust me? (one promise kept each day) | **Built**, against the app's own definition. |
| Autonomy | Real choice; reasons I endorse | Commitment Statement, What guides my choices? | **Built.** |
| Self-advocacy | Knowing what I need, and practising saying it | Nothing | **No activity.** *Say What You Need* (13 November). |
| Self-authorship | Looking at where a belief came from | Where You've Come From | **Built.** |

Activities in these levels that build none of the nine: What Matters, What They Care About, The Contribution Map, Who Else Cares. All four build purpose.

**A note on one try-it.** Self-discipline's page says "Pick one 10-minute habit and do it five days in a row. Tick it off somewhere you can see." In the one careful study of habit formation, the middle time to a habit was about 66 days, in a small adult sample (Lally et al., 2010). Five days is a fair trial of a routine. It isn't a habit yet, and "five days in a row" is close to a streak, which the app otherwise avoids.

## 5. Meaning ("What kind of life do I want?")

*Imagine myself:* Actual self · Ideal self · Ought self · Feared self · Possible selves
*Authentic self:* Authentic self

### 5.1 What research says meaning is made of

| Framework | Its parts | How the six selves match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meaning in life** (Martela & Steger, 2016). | Coherence (my life makes sense), purpose (it has direction), significance (it is worth living; I matter) | Direction is here (ideal and possible selves). **Coherence has no self.** Significance is Mattering, which sits in Connection. |
| **Self-discrepancy theory** (Higgins, 1987). | Actual, ideal and ought selves. A gap between actual and ideal goes with feeling flat; a gap between actual and ought goes with feeling anxious. | Actual, ideal and ought self, exactly. |
| **Possible selves** (Markus & Nurius, 1986; Oyserman & Markus, 1990). | Hoped-for, expected and feared selves | Possible selves, feared self. |
| **Authenticity** (Wood et al., 2008; Kernis & Goldman, 2006). | Living by my values; not just going along with others; not feeling out of touch with myself | Authentic self. |

### 5.2 Each self

| Self | Research standing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual self | Strong, but it **is** self-concept | Higgins's actual self is the self-concept under another name. It earns its place here only as the starting point the other four are measured from. |
| Ideal self | Strong | Also the "hoped-for" possible self. |
| Ought self | Strong | Handle with care (5.4). |
| Feared self | Moderate | Most useful when paired with a hoped-for self in the same area (Oyserman & Markus, 1990). |
| Possible selves | Strong, including one school trial | The level's five selves come from two theories laid side by side. Ideal and feared are also possible selves, so Possible selves is the heading the others sit under as much as a fifth item. |
| Authentic self | Moderate to strong | |

### 5.3 What's missing

| Gap | Why it belongs here | What the app already has | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **My story** (coherence) | The first of the three parts of meaning. | The Through-Line; the Character Code's "story in one sentence"; Where You've Come From | The same gap as in Know myself. **One self would serve both.** Know myself fits "my story so far"; Meaning fits "how it hangs together". I'd put it in Know myself and have The Through-Line point back to it. |
| The kind of person I want to be for others (moral identity) | The earlier review called this the research backbone of the character half. | The Character Code; The Standard | No new self. Self-respect and Authentic self carry it between them. Worth saying so on those pages. |

### 5.4 Do the activities build each self?

| Self | What research suggests builds it | What the level has now | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Actual self | An honest description of now | Digital Self was placed here for it in D53. It is about the gap between online and offline, which is self-image. | **No activity.** Draft: *You Are Here*. Or accept that Know myself builds it, and say so on the page. |
| Ideal self | Writing about your best possible future (Carrillo et al., 2019: small to moderate gains in optimism and mood across 29 studies, mostly adults) | Who am I becoming?, A Life Worth Building | **Built.** |
| Ought self | Sorting expectations into ones I hold as mine and ones I don't | Nothing here. Who is shaping me? (Know myself) is nearby. | **No activity.** Draft: *Should or Choose?* |
| Feared self | Naming it alongside the hoped-for opposite, and one step toward that | Who am I becoming? asks what they never want to become, in passing | **Touched.** Draft: *The Other Version*. |
| Possible selves | Picturing futures **and** tying each to something to do now. In Oyserman, Bybee & Terry (2006), a randomised trial with Year 8 students, grades and attendance improved for two years, and the effect ran through that link to present action. | Future Self, How They Chose | **Built.** Worth checking that Future Self ends with a step for this term. |
| Authentic self | Acting on my values in a room where I usually don't | The Through-Line, One Deliberate Choice, the Character Code | **Built.** |

**Two cautions for this level.**
- **Ought self.** Making the gap between who I am and who I'm expected to be more vivid can raise anxiety; that is what Higgins's theory predicts. The activity should sort expectations, not measure the gap. It should also leave room for expectations a student is glad to keep. Many students hold commitments from home that are theirs (D71).
- **Feared self.** It has to stay in everyday territory (the version of me who gives up, who is unkind) and always travel with the hoped-for opposite. Both drafts need a counsellor's read before they go near students.

## 6. Across the whole map

### 6.1 Selves that overlap

These pairs are close enough that a student, or a teacher, could fairly ask what the difference is. Each page's "not this" line does the separating, and that is the right place for it.

| Pair | What research says |
| --- | --- |
| Self-esteem and self-worth | The same construct in most research. The app's split rests on Crocker and Kernis. |
| Self-efficacy and self-confidence | Confidence is the everyday word for efficacy. |
| Self-regulation and self-discipline | Often used for the same thing. |
| Actual self and self-concept | The same thing in two theories. |
| Ideal self, feared self and possible selves | The first two are kinds of the third. |
| Autonomy, self-authorship and authentic self | Three angles on "is this really mine?" |
