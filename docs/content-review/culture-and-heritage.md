# Culture and Heritage: wording for community review

**Status:** draft, live in the app as an optional extra, not yet reviewed by community members.
**Where it is:** Mission 3 (Connection), listed with the optional extras. It's never counted towards finishing the mission and never suggested on Home.
**Code:** the step is `culture-and-heritage` in `src/lib/missions.ts`; its sentence starters and hints are in `src/lib/missionScaffolds.ts`.

## Why it exists

Cultural and ethnic identity is one of the best-evidenced parts of how young people build a sense of who they are. Exploring your background, and feeling good about it, is linked to wellbeing (Umaña-Taylor and colleagues; Rivas-Drake and colleagues). Before this step, Groundwork only touched culture through three items in Mission 4's "keep, rework, leave" sort and an optional family-story conversation.

The wording below was written by the developer alone. It should be reviewed, and changed, by people from the communities it speaks to before it's promoted anywhere.

## How it handles the student's answers

- Every question can be left out with one tap, and leaving one out still counts the step as done.
- What a student writes is never sent to the AI, and never appears on My story.
- In the Journal it's collapsed behind "Private · tap to show".
- It's never suggested for a revisit on Home.
- The on-device crisis check still runs. If it matches, the student sees the support card, which includes 13YARN.

## Suggested reviewers

- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander educators, Elders or community members, for anything touching Country, kin and culture, and disconnection from culture.
- Multicultural youth workers, and young people from migrant and refugee backgrounds.
- Young people of mixed heritage, and young people who'd describe themselves as having "no culture" or as "just Australian".
- Someone with experience of young people in out-of-home care or adoption.

## The wording

### Intro (shown before the questions)

> Culture can mean where your family is from, a language, a faith, food, Country, traditions, or just the way things are done at your place. You might have one, several, be finding yours, or feel you don't have one. All of those are real places to start. Every question can be left out, and none of it is a test. What you write stays in your account and is never sent anywhere. If something's hard, Get help lists people to talk to, including 13YARN for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people.

**For reviewers:**
- Does any group feel left out or singled out?
- Is "Country" used appropriately here, or should connection to Country be named differently or separately?
- Should this say anything directly to students who've been cut off from their culture, for example through adoption, care or history? Or would that do more harm than good in a self-guided app?

### The four questions

1. In your own words, where are you and your family from? It might be one culture, a few, a mix, a faith community, Country, a place, or something you're still working out.
2. What's something from your culture or background that you're proud of, or that feels good to be part of? A person, a tradition, a language, food, a way of seeing things.
3. Is there anywhere you feel you have to explain, play down or hide that part of you? What's that like, and where can you just be it?
4. What would you like to know or understand better about where you're from? Who could you learn it from?

**For reviewers:**
- Question 3 can bring up experiences of racism. Is it safe to ask in a self-guided app with no adult in the room? Is the hint for it (below) enough?
- Does question 4 assume someone is available to learn from? What's a good answer for a student who has nobody to ask?

### Sentence starters (one set per question)

1. "My family is from…", "I'd describe my background as…", "I'm still working out…"
2. "Something I'm proud of is…", "I feel part of it when…", "Something we do that I love is…"
3. "I play it down when…", "I don't have to explain it with…", "I can just be it when…"
4. "I'd like to know more about…", "Someone who could tell me is…", "I could start by…"

### Hints (shown when a student taps "Stuck? Try this")

1. Mixed, unsure and "just Australian" are all real answers. / You could start with a grandparent, a place, a language or a food.
2. It can be small: a recipe, a word, a song, the way your family celebrates. / Who in your family or community carries it?
3. If someone has treated you badly because of your background, that's on them, not you. / Think in rooms, like the Mask Check: which ones, and with whom?
4. A relative, an Elder, a community group, a language class, a library. / One question to one person is a real start.

**For reviewers:**
- Is "just Australian" the right phrase to name, or does it carry meanings we don't intend?
- Should hint 3 also point to support, as well as the Get help button that's always on screen?

### Shown after finishing

> However much you know about where you're from, you've just thought about it on purpose. That's how a background you were handed starts to become something you've chosen. Plenty of people keep learning about theirs their whole lives.

### "Why it matters" (one tap away)

> Research with young people from many backgrounds, including work by Adriana Umaña-Taylor and colleagues, finds that exploring your cultural or ethnic background and feeling good about it go along with stronger self-esteem and wellbeing, and some studies find it helps young people cope with being treated unfairly. It isn't about how much you already know or how cultural you seem. It's about taking an interest and deciding what it means to you.

## Open questions

- **Placement:** is Mission 3 (belonging) the right home, or should it sit with Parts of Who You Are in Mission 1 (identity)?
- **Prominence:** should it stay an optional extra, or be offered more prominently to students who choose it?
- **Support services:** are 13YARN and the general lines (Kids Helpline, Lifeline) enough, or should the help list name a service for racism or for multicultural young people?
