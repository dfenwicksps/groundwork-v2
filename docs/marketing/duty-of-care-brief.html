Groundwork and your duty of care

Groundwork doesn't change your school's duty of care, and it can't carry any of it. Here's what the app does when a student is struggling, and how it uses AI, so you can tell students and families accurately.

Nobody at school can see what students write

Every answer is visible only to the student who wrote it. There's no teacher or parent view, no alerts and no per-class report. The most a school can see is completion totals across all students, with counts under five hidden.

So Groundwork is not a disclosure channel. A student who writes something worrying in it has told no one.

What it does when a student may be at risk

Mission steps, weekly reflections, revisits and Your Next Chapter are checked on the student's own device for phrases that suggest risk (goals, the practice log and the Character Code aren't yet). If one matches, the app shows free services (Kids Helpline, Lifeline, 13YARN, QLife) in place of its usual questions. If several recent entries are very hard on themselves, it suggests talking to someone.

Nothing is saved, flagged or sent to anyone. It puts help in front of the student, not an adult. The phrase list is English only and misses a lot. Don't describe it as monitoring.

What stays with the school
  • Say it when you introduce it: Groundwork is private, it isn't a way to tell the school anything, and here is who you can tell.
  • Keep your pastoral and referral processes as they are. Groundwork is universal, tier 1 material that sits inside them. It doesn't replace support for a student who needs more.
  • Brief the staff who introduce it, so nobody promises a teacher view or follow-up that doesn't exist.
  • Tell families. Students must be 13 or older, and under 16 we ask that a parent or carer knows about the account.
  • Check it against your sector's rules for online services, student data and generative AI before students sign up.

What
When a student finishes a reflection, the first 500 or so characters are sent to Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. It writes three follow-up questions, and the same request checks for signs of risk. Anthropic doesn't train its models on this content.
Told
The step says so before the student taps Finish. The questions are labelled Suggested by AI wherever they appear, and Settings and the privacy page explain it.
Choice
It's on by default. One switch in Settings turns it off, and then nothing a student writes leaves Groundwork's database.
Never sent
The optional steps on beliefs, gender and sexuality and on culture and heritage, whatever the setting.
Limits
The AI only writes questions. It doesn't give advice, hold a conversation or act as a counsellor, and no person reviews what it writes. Everything else in the app is fixed text, not generated.
