GGroundwork

What your teenager will actually be doing

Groundwork is a self-guided programme that helps teenagers work out who they are — and then asks them to go and act like it. Four missions, ten weeks, and one page they write themselves at the end. Here is what that means at home.

How it works

Four missions ask one big question each — who am I, what do I care about, where do I belong, what kind of life do I want. Each is a few short steps and an optional chat with someone — maybe you.

Ten weeks then turn those answers into practice. One question a week, and one thing to actually do about it — away from the screen.

What it isn't

  • Not social. No feed, no followers, no profile, nothing public.
  • Not scored. No marks, no streaks, no comparison with other students.
  • Not demanding. Nothing expires and nothing nags. A missed week is not a failed programme.
Some of the things they'll be asked to do — in the programme's own words
  1. Week 3Do one useful thing nobody asked you to do — and tell nobody.
  2. Week 4Make one small promise and keep it every single day this week.
  3. Week 5Pick one personal hill and work on it four times. Not perfectly. Consistently.
  4. Week 8Name three peers who lift you and two adults worth learning from — then go and spend real time with them.
  5. Week 9Three half-hours with no screen at all. Boredom is the point, not the problem.
  6. Week 10Write your Character Code: your story in one sentence, then your commitments for the year.

What you won't see

Nothing your teenager writes is visible to you, or to their teachers. That is a deliberate design decision, not a setting someone forgot to change — and it is the reason the answers are honest. There is no parent view and no teacher dashboard, by design.

What it is not for

Groundwork is not a therapy app and never claims to be. It says so to students directly, and a "Need to talk?" button on every screen lists free services, including Kids Helpline on 1800 55 1800. If something is heavy, it sends them to a person — not back into the app.

The one thing you will see

At the end of the ten weeks your teenager writes a Character Code: their story in one sentence, then five to seven commitments, written as things they do, not things they'd like to be. It's theirs to share or not — but it is the single best thing to ask about.

Three ways to help

“

What's the challenge this week?

Better than "how's the app going?" — it asks about an action, which is easier to answer than a feeling.

“

Week 8 asks you to name two adults worth learning from. Did I make the list?

Ask it lightly. The week is about deliberate time with people who shape you — you may already be one of them.

“

Don't tell me what you wrote — just tell me if it was hard.

Signals that you understand the privacy is the point, which makes them far more likely to volunteer the rest.
