# Groundwork: curriculum and wellbeing alignment

**What this is:** a map of Groundwork's content against two Australian documents a school will ask about:
- the **Australian Curriculum, Version 9.0**: Health and Physical Education (HPE) for Years 7–8 and 9–10, plus three general capabilities;
- the **Australian Student Wellbeing Framework** (2018): its five elements and their effective practices.

It sits beside [`frameworks.md`](frameworks.md), which maps the same content to the research and to CASEL. That document covers *why* each activity is built the way it is. This one covers *where it fits in a school*.

**Checked against:** the curriculum codes and wording were read from australiancurriculum.edu.au, and the Wellbeing Framework's effective practices from the Student Wellbeing Hub, on 8 October 2026. Check them again before putting a code in front of a school, since ACARA does revise.

---

## 1. Read this first

- **Groundwork supports curriculum content. It doesn't deliver it.** The curriculum describes what teachers teach and assess. Groundwork is self-guided, has no teacher view and no marking, and nobody but the student can read their writing. So the honest claim is "this activity gives students practice in what AC9HP10P01 describes", not "this covers AC9HP10P01".
- **Teachers can't use it as assessment evidence on their own.** The one exception is student choice: *My Story* is built to be printed or shown to someone, so a student can bring it to a teacher. It deliberately leaves out *Parts of Who You Are* and *Culture and Heritage*.
- **The F–10 curriculum stops at Year 10.** Groundwork runs from Year 7 to a few years after school. Year 11–12 health and wellbeing courses belong to each state's senior secondary authority (QCAA, NESA, VCAA and so on) and aren't mapped here.
- **Only the Personal, social and community health strand is mapped.** Groundwork has nothing for the Movement and physical activity strand, and shouldn't be described as an HPE resource without that qualifier.
- **Two ratings are used.** **Direct**: the activity's main purpose is what the content description asks for. **Partial**: the activity touches one part of it, or does it through reflection only, without the knowledge content a teacher would add.

## 2. The short version

| | Strongest fit | Weakest fit |
|---|---|---|
| **HPE content** | Identities and change: AC9HP8P01, AC9HP10P01 (identity, values, who shapes you); AC9HP8P02, AC9HP10P02 (change and transitions, via Your Next Chapter and Mission 4) | Gender stereotypes and violence (P03), consent (P07), and critiquing health information (P09). Nothing for the Movement strand. |
| **General capabilities** | Personal and Social capability, especially *Self-awareness* and *Self-management*. Ethical Understanding, especially *Make and reflect on ethical decisions*. | Social management (communication, collaboration, conflict resolution): practised through the conversation steps, but not taught. |
| **Wellbeing Framework** | **Student voice** and **Support**: strengths-based, evidence-informed, explicit work on social and emotional skills and help-seeking | **Leadership**: Groundwork gives school leaders no data, by design. Partnerships are only at the family level. |

The one-line version for a school: *Groundwork gives students regular, private practice in the identity and wellbeing parts of the HPE Personal, social and community health strand and the Personal and Social capability, and fits the Student voice and Support elements of the Wellbeing Framework.*

## 3. HPE: Personal, social and community health

The content descriptions are quoted from v9. Years 7–8 and 9–10 sit side by side because Groundwork serves both bands with the same activities. The year level changes examples and order (see `stageContent.ts` and `spine.ts`), not the curriculum focus.

### Identities and change

| Code | Content description | Where Groundwork does it | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AC9HP8P01** | analyse and reflect on the influence of values and beliefs on the development of identities | Values Clarifier (M1); Program week 2, *What do I stand for?*; Parts of Who You Are (M1, optional: beliefs); What You Were Handed (sorting what family and culture gave you); Commitment Statement (M2) | **Direct** |
| **AC9HP10P01** | analyse factors that shape identities and evaluate how individuals influence the identities of others | The Mask Check (M1); The People Who Shaped You (M3); Who Was I? (M1 conversation); Program week 8, *Who is shaping me?*; Fitting In vs. Belonging (M3); stories *The Version of Me at School* and *What the Mirror Doesn't Show* | **Direct** |
| **AC9HP8P02** | analyse the impact of changes and transitions, and devise strategies to support themselves and others through these changes | Your Story So Far (M1, life as chapters); Where You've Come From (M4); Your Next Chapter (subject choice for Years 10–11, next year at school for Years 7–9); story *The Friend Who Stayed* (parents separating, a friend who shows up) | **Direct** |
| **AC9HP10P02** | refine, evaluate and adapt strategies for managing changes and transitions | Your Next Chapter (hoped-for and feared versions of next year, each with a concrete step); Future Self (M4); How They Chose (M4 conversation); Pathways; Goals (wish, outcome, obstacle, plan); Revisits (comparing what you wrote months apart) | **Direct** |
| **AC9HP8P03** / **AC9HP10P03** | gender stereotypes in relationships; gender equality and preventing violence and abuse | Parts of Who You Are asks how gender sits with what people expect. Nothing about power, stereotypes or violence. | **Gap** |

### Interacting with others

| Code | Content description | Where Groundwork does it | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AC9HP8P04** / **AC9HP10P04** | the roles of respect, empathy, power and coercion in respectful relationships | The Standard, Q1 (*How am I making others feel safer and stronger?*) and its counterweight (*who feels less safe around me?*); Program week 4, *Can people trust me?*; Empathy and Compassion Boosts; Habit Check, *Reading how others feel* | **Partial**: respect and empathy yes; power and coercion not addressed |
| **AC9HP8P05** / **AC9HP10P05** | value diversity; challenge biases, stereotypes, prejudices and discrimination; promote inclusion | Across the Gap (M3, bridging relationships across real differences); Culture and Heritage (M3, optional, including where students feel they have to hide that part of themselves); Habit Check, *Standing up for others*; story *Different Enough* | **Partial**: reflective and personal; doesn't teach about bias or discrimination as such |
| **AC9HP8P06** / **AC9HP10P06** | emotional responses and strategies to self-manage emotions | The Mask Check (what you were protecting, what the fear costs); the Seven Selves (telling self-esteem from self-worth, so a bad day isn't treated as a verdict); *Being hard on yourself* card; Resilience Boost; Program week 9 (screen-free reflection) | **Partial**: emotional awareness is strong; regulation strategies are light |
| **AC9HP8P07** / **AC9HP10P07** | assertive communication; seeking, giving and denying consent | Habit Check, *Facing hard conversations*, touches assertive communication. Nothing on consent. | **Gap** |

### Making healthy and safe choices

| Code | Content description | Where Groundwork does it | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AC9HP8P08** | refine protective behaviours and evaluate community resources to seek help for themselves and others | Get help (Kids Helpline, Lifeline, 13YARN, QLife, chosen so every age 13–23 has one that's plainly for them); the on-device crisis phrase check; the support card shown after writing; Support Circle | **Direct** for help-seeking; protective behaviours not taught |
| **AC9HP10P08** | plan, rehearse and evaluate strategies for situations where health, safety or wellbeing may be at risk | Same help surfaces as above. No rehearsal of risky situations. | **Partial** |
| **AC9HP8P09** | how media and influential people impact attitudes, beliefs, decisions and behaviours | Digital Self (M4: what your feed reflects back at you, who you are online versus offline); Program week 8 (who is shaping you) | **Partial** |
| **AC9HP10P09** | critique health information, services and media messaging | Digital Self touches media messaging. No critique of health information. | **Partial**, weak |
| **AC9HP8P10** / **AC9HP10P10** | plan strategies to enhance their own and others' health, safety, relationships and wellbeing | The 10-Week Program as a whole: each week ends in a lived challenge (one unnoticed contribution a day, one promise kept, three screen-free half hours). The Standard. Boosts, each with an exercise for this week. Goals. | **Direct** for *own and others' wellbeing and relationships*; not health or safety |

**HPE focus areas.** Of v9's focus areas, Groundwork sits within **mental health and wellbeing** and, through *Parts of Who You Are*, touches **relationships and sexuality** at the level of identity only. It isn't a relationships and sexuality education resource.

**Against the achievement standards.** Year 10: "propose and evaluate personal strategies to manage their identities, emotions and responses to change" is the closest single sentence to what Groundwork does. Year 8: "analyse factors that influence identities, emotions and responses to change" and "help-seeking strategies applied online and offline" both fit. Neither standard's sentences on assertive communication, protective behaviours or credible health information are covered.

### Intercultural Understanding

| Element | Sub-element | Where Groundwork develops it | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reflecting on culture and cultural diversity** | Reflect on the relationship between cultures and identities | Culture and Heritage (M3, optional); Where We Come From (M3 conversation); What You Were Handed | **Direct**, but see below |
| | Examine cultural perspectives and worldviews | Parts of Who You Are (beliefs, optional) | **Partial** |
| **Engaging with cultural and linguistic diversity** | Develop multiple perspectives / Develop empathy | Across the Gap; story *Different Enough* | **Partial** |

**Caveat on culture.** Culture and Heritage was drafted without community input and is waiting on the review planned in [`content-review/culture-and-heritage.md`](content-review/culture-and-heritage.md). Until that review is done, don't claim alignment with the **Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures** cross-curriculum priority, even though the step mentions Country and Get help lists 13YARN.

Critical and Creative Thinking isn't mapped. Students do reflect, but Groundwork doesn't set out to teach thinking skills, and claiming it would stretch.

## 5. Australian Student Wellbeing Framework

The Framework is written for whole schools: principals, staff, families. An app for individual students can only support the effective practices that involve students directly. The table rates each effective practice, worded as on the Student Wellbeing Hub, as **Supports**, **Partial** or **Not Groundwork's to do**.

### Student voice: *authentic student participation*

| Effective practice | Groundwork | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Provide opportunities for authentic student decision-making over matters that affect them | Students choose their own values, commitments, hills and Character Code. Nothing is gated: the year-level spine recommends a track but never blocks the other. The scaffolding tier is the student's choice and persists. | **Supports** |
| Create and maintain inclusive and interactive learning environments to encourage active student participation | Quick / Extended / Open scaffolding on every prompt, so students who freeze at a blank page can still take part. Optional steps on sensitive topics, with every question skippable. | **Supports** |
| Actively engage students through the use of evidence-informed, strengths-based approaches | VIA character strengths at the centre; every activity tied to named research (see [`frameworks.md`](frameworks.md)). No scores, no streaks. | **Supports**: Groundwork's strongest match |
| Explicitly teach social and emotional skills using evidence-informed practices related to personal safety, resilience, help-seeking and protective behaviours | Resilience Boost; Get help and the support card; the on-device crisis check. Protective behaviours and personal safety aren't taught. | **Partial** |
| Collaborate with students to develop strategies to enhance wellbeing, promote safety and counter violence, bullying and abuse in all online and physical spaces | Digital Self looks at online life, and the device lock protects a student on a shared device. Nothing on bullying or violence. The phrase-list co-design planned in [`content-review/phrase-lists.md`](content-review/phrase-lists.md) would add collaboration with students. | **Partial** |

### Support: *wellbeing and support for positive behaviour*

| Effective practice | Groundwork | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Actively seek the involvement of school staff, students and families in the promotion and recognition of positive behaviour | The Standard and Program week 3 build positive behaviour, but deliberately without recognition: week 3 forbids mentioning the contribution. | **Partial**, and by design |
| Embed wellbeing and support for positive behaviour strategies that are evidence-informed, promote resilience and align with the needs of the school community | The 10-Week Program is a ready-made sequence a school can run alongside its own programme, with lived challenges each week. | **Supports** |
| Implement a whole school systemic approach to wellbeing … with tiers of support | Groundwork is a universal (tier 1) resource. It points to help services but is not a targeted or intensive support and shouldn't be positioned as one. | **Partial**: tier 1 only |
| Engage in professional learning … | — | **Not Groundwork's to do** |
| Critically analyse and evaluate school data to inform decision-making | No data goes to the school. Answers are shown only to the student (privacy page). The pilot in [`evidence/evidence-plan.md`](evidence/evidence-plan.md) is the route to evidence a school can use. | **Not Groundwork's to do**, by design |

### Inclusion: *inclusive and connected school culture*

| Effective practice | Groundwork | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Respect the diversity of the school community … build a cohesive and culturally safe school | Culture and Heritage and Parts of Who You Are (optional, never sent to AI, left out of My Story); wording for students who've left school or are in Years 7–9; every *Talk to someone* step offers a non-family alternative | **Partial**: needs the community review first |
| Teach, model and promote values and behaviour | Values Clarifier, the Program, Character Code | **Supports** |
| Foster and maintain positive, caring and respectful … relationships | The Standard; Program week 8; Across the Gap | **Partial**: student–peer only |
| Recognise contribution of staff, students and families; professional learning | — | **Not Groundwork's to do** |

### Partnerships: *effective family and community partnerships*

| Effective practice | Groundwork | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Proactively build collaborative relationships with families and communities | The four *Talk to someone* steps send students to a parent, grandparent, Elder or community member. That builds family connection, not school–family partnership. Under 16, the privacy page asks that a parent or carer knows about the account. | **Partial** |
| Build partnerships with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander families and community organisations | Not yet. The culture review is where this would start. | **Not yet** |
| Other partnership practices (community services, location-specific needs) | Help services listed are national, not local. | **Not Groundwork's to do** |

### Leadership: *visible leadership*

Every Leadership practice belongs to principals and school leaders. Groundwork's contribution is indirect: it can be part of the school's wellbeing plan, and its design choices (no teacher can read a journal; nothing is reported to the school) are things leaders need to understand and communicate. It gives leaders no monitoring data, and that's deliberate.

## 7. Gaps, and whether to close them

| Gap | Close it? |
|---|---|
| Consent (P07) and gender, power and violence (P03) | **No.** These need a teacher, a class and a school's respectful relationships programme. Done badly in a private app, they could do harm. Say plainly that Groundwork doesn't cover them. |
| Movement and physical activity strand | **No.** Out of scope. |
| Protective behaviours and rehearsing risky situations (P08) | Probably not. Same reasoning as consent. Help-seeking stays. |
| Critiquing health information and media (P09) | **Maybe.** A second Digital Self step on how a feed sells you a version of yourself would fit the app's voice and close most of 8P09. |
| Emotional regulation strategies (P06) | **Maybe.** A *Steady* Boost with one regulation exercise would sit beside Resilience. |
| Social management: leadership, collaboration | Low priority. These are learned with other people, which Groundwork deliberately isn't. |
| Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures (CCP) and partnerships | **Yes**, through the community review that's already planned, not by adding claims first. |

## 8. What to say, and what not to

**Say:**
- "Groundwork gives students practice in the Identities and change content of the HPE curriculum: AC9HP8P01 and P02 in Years 7–8, AC9HP10P01 and P02 in Years 9–10."
- "It develops the Self-awareness and Self-management elements of the Personal and Social capability, and Program week 6 and the Moral Compass work directly on Ethical Understanding."
- "It fits the Student voice and Support elements of the Australian Student Wellbeing Framework: strengths-based, evidence-informed, and student-directed."

**Don't say:**
- "Covers" or "delivers" any content description. Groundwork isn't taught or assessed.
- "Aligned to the HPE curriculum" without saying *Personal, social and community health*.
- Anything implying it handles consent, respectful relationships education or protective behaviours.
- Anything about the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cross-curriculum priority before the community review.
- That it helps schools monitor wellbeing. It reports nothing to the school, and that's the point.
